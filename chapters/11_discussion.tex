\subsection{DNA Cloning}
DNA cloning was concluded by sequencing of the plasmids and the calculation of the yield from the midi-preparation. The full agreement of the sequencing result with the designed sequences enables further use of the plasmids in cell-free expression. The yields of the midi-preparation were slightly lower than the manufacturer's reported typical yield of \SI{500}{\micro\gram}, but still high enough to allow for use in cell-free expression after concentration. 

\subsection{Protein Expression}
\subsubsection{Strep-eYFP Yield by Fluorescence}
Following cell-free expression, the protein yield of the batch was estimated by fluorescence measurements of the Strep-eYFP reaction setup. The determined concentration of \SI{0.3}{\milli\gram\per\milli\litre} was significantly lower than the documented yields of up to \SI{3}{\milli\gram\per\milli\litre} \cite{alice_system}. Typical reasons for low lysate performance, as stated by the manufacturer, are an inappropriate amount of plasmid DNA, poor oxygenation, or expired lysate. As for the plasmid DNA, the manufacturer explains that the optimal amount varies for each protein and should be determined by testing. The plasmid concentration of \SI{50}{\nano\Molar} used in this work was chosen based on experience at the Institute of Molecular Biotechnology (RWTH Aachen University) and exceeded the amount suggested by the manufacturer. Potentially, use of less DNA could improve the yield. Oxygenation in this setup was provided through a hole in the reaction tube's lid. An optimal oxygen supply could be established by using the dedicated perforated lids shipped with newer versions of the lysate. Use of newer lysate might also lead to a higher yield, since deterioration of the kit's components is a known issue. The ALiCE\textsuperscript{\textregistered} reaction mixes used in this work were past their expiration date and were kindly provided to the institute free of charge. The low yield of Strep-eYFP indicates a low expression of the constructs, which might have had a negative impact on complex assembly.

\subsubsection{Coomassie Staining and Western Blot}
Evaluation of the cell-free expression's qualitative success, as well as of the Capto\textsuperscript{\texttrademark} Core chromatography, was conducted through Western blots using an anti-PVX antibody and an anti-S-Tag antibody.

After the Coomassie staining of the ALiCE\textsuperscript{\textregistered} reaction mixture, the samples d28A, S-Tag-A, and S-Tag-B, showed no notable difference from the non-template control, while the tracks containing expression mix from the PVX CP and the Strep-eYFP setup displayed bands at molecular weights matching the control. This could imply a lower yield in these setups. However, the bands from other proteins in the lysate provided bad contrast, and visual differences might simply be caused by the fact that the bands were at slightly different heights than those of Strep-eYFP, and possibly worse to distinguish from the background. Further, the control S-Tag-CP PVX only displayed a faint band as well, reinforcing that there is no sure implication on the yield. 

The anti-PVX Western blot showed defined bands for d28A, S-Tag-A, and PVX CP, but only a very faint band for S-Tag-B. This could be due to a lower yield for S-Tag-B, or less avidity of the anti-PVX antibody against the protein, given that it only has \SI{53}{\percent} sequence identity to the wild type. For all samples and the control S-Tag-CP PVX, the migration distance suggested a molecular weight higher than the theoretical one. For PVX particles from plants, this is a well-known phenomenon and partly due to specific glycosylations of the proteins \cite{pvx_glycosylation}. However, glycosylation of the ALiCE\textsuperscript{\textregistered} samples is unlikely, since they were expressed through the cytosolic pathway. The discrepancy with the theoretical weights might imply partial aggregation. Binding of the anti-PVX antibody against d28A and S-Tag-A and the faint signal for S-Tag-B indicates that the designs maintain some epitopes of the polyclonal anti-PVX antibody. 

In the Western blot using the anti-S-Tag antibody, both samples S-Tag-A and S-Tag-B showed a strong signal, implying that the antibody has high avidity against them. The signal was not constrained to the migration distance as seen in the anti-PVX blot, but was faintly visible through most of the track. Signal at larger molecular weights might have stemmed from aggregates, while signal at lower molecular weights suggests the existence of smaller protein parts containing an S-Tag, possibly through partial degradation of the protein while boiling the samples. 

Following Capto\textsuperscript{\texttrademark} Core purification, no discernible bands were visible in the Coomassie gel or the Western blot. The signals in the first iteration of the Western blot likely arose from column resin permeating the filter and carrying protein with it. However, it is also possible that the signal in the first batch actually stemmed from specific or unspecific protein complexes that did not form in the second batch. This is rendered unlikely since, in the first Western blot, the wild type PVX CP also showed a signal, even though it is known to not assemble in absence of its genome \cite{juli_sagt_keine_assembly}. In any case, the Western blot following Capto\textsuperscript{\texttrademark} Core purification indicates that there were little to no assembled complexes in the reaction mixture. 

\subsubsection{ELISA}
For a quantitative analysis of the cell-free expression of d28A, S-Tag-A, S-Tag-B, and PVX CP, ELISAs using both anti-PVX and anti-S-Tag antibodies were conducted. 

In both cases, the optical densities of the samples were almost identical for the 1:500 and the 1:1000 dilution. This might be caused by other proteins in the lysate saturating the binding capacity of the microplate, even at the stronger dilution, and thus rendering only the proportion of the specific protein in the reaction mixture relevant. Under this assumption, the result from the 1:1000 dilution would be more significant, even though in general stronger dilutions should be tested to get a better estimate. 

Using the 1:1000 dilution, the highest protein concentration in the anti-PVX ELISA was observed for the PVX CP, with a calculated concentration of \SI{134}{\micro\gram\per\milli\litre}. This is less than half the yield measured for Strep-eYFP expression by the lysate. A possible cause is lower expression or quicker degradation of the protein in the reaction mixture, since the lysate is optimized for production of Strep-eYFP \cite{alice_system}. A different reason could be that the antibody has higher avidity for the assembled viral particles than for the PVX CP in the ALiCE\textsuperscript{\textregistered} expression mix, which are known to not assemble without specific RNA present \cite{pvx_assembly}. This is plausible, since the antibody was generated by rabbit immunization against viral particles. For the constructs d28A, S-Tag-A, and S-Tag-B, the anti-PVX ELISA lead to lower calculated concentrations of about \SI{30}{\micro\gram\per\milli\litre} for d28A and S-Tag-A, and no discernible signal for S-Tag-B. This could also be due to lower avidity of the antibody towards the adapted sequences. The missing signal for S-Tag-B is consistent with the anti-PVX Western blot, which showed only a very faint line for S-Tag-B. 

Using the anti-S-Tag antibody, only the samples from S-Tag-A and S-Tag-B had significant optical densities. The back-calculated concentrations of \SI{2.4}{\milli\gram\per\milli\litre} and \SI{1.2}{\milli\gram\per\milli\litre} are very high, and unlikely given the measured expression of only \SI{0.3}{\milli\gram\per\milli\litre} for the Strep-eYFP ALiCE\textsuperscript{\textregistered} control setup. Possibly, the anti-S-Tag antibody has considerably higher avidity against protein monomers than for the assembled particles in the calibration samples. This could explain the high optical density, since the faint signal in the Capto\textsuperscript{\texttrademark} Core after the Western blot implies that little to no protein in the ALiCE\textsuperscript{\textregistered} setup assembled. The high signal might also be due to the fact that the anti-S-Tag antibody used is not explicitly labeled as being suitable for ELISA, even though the calibration showed a linear trend with high determination.

Because of the potential differences in antibody avidity between the control and the constructs, the exact yield of the constructs could not be determined. Still, the results suggest that the constructs d28A and S-Tag-A were expressed with yields of at least \SI{10}{\percent} relative to Strep-eYFP, and S-Tag-B could be expressed in substantial amounts. Expression of Strep-eYFP in ALiCE\textsuperscript{\textregistered} is known to be scalable to reaction volumes up to \SI{1}{\litre} with no significant difference in yield \cite{alice_system}. These findings highlight the potential of scaling up the ALiCE\textsuperscript{\textregistered} system for the designs used in this experiment, enabling a straightforward transition to production.

\subsubsection{Electron Microscopy}
The electron microscopy, conducted to analyze whether assembled particles were present in the purified ALiCE\textsuperscript{\textregistered} reaction mixture, showed no signs of VLP formation for the constructs d28A, S-Tag-A, as well as the expressed PVX CP. The visible dark clumps in the images also appeared in the non-template control and are thus likely not related to the specific expressed protein, but rather to other components of the lysate that passed the Capto\textsuperscript{\texttrademark} Core column. This observation is in line with the absence of a signal in the Western blots after the purification. 

The control sample of S-Tag-CP PVX virions clearly showed the filamentous, flexible structure of PVX virions as described by Grinzato et al. (2020) \cite{Grinzato2020}. The measured size of \SI{17}{\nano\meter} in diameter and about ~\SI{600}{\nano\meter} in length agrees with values from literature of a diameter of \SI{15}{\nano\meter} and a modal length of ~\SI{500}{\nano\meter}. 

One of the two rod-like structures found in the purified reaction mix of S-Tag-B also has the shape of PVX virions, with a measured diameter of \SI{15}{\nano\meter}, a length of \SI{760}{\nano\meter}, and a flexible structure. The other structure was thicker than PVX, with a measured diameter of \SI{24}{\nano\meter}. Both could potentially indicate assembly of S-Tag-B coat proteins into helical complexes. However, the fact that only two of those structures were found in the sample make this result unsure. Clarification on whether these structures were actually made up of S-Tag-B monomers could be achieved by using immunoelectron microscopy (exemplary image in \autoref{fig:em_immuno_labeling}).
